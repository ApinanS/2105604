Why adsorption & desorption count as chemical steps

It is chemisorption — real bonds form

A reactant does not merely touch the surface; it forms a chemical bond with an active site (chemisorption).
  • Physisorption = weak van der Waals — reversible, no reaction.
  • Chemisorption = a surface chemical bond — this is a reaction, so its rate is a chemical rate.
  • Example: ethylidene chemisorbed on platinum (Pt).

Grouping the seven steps

Two families of steps

Chemical rate steps

3 · Adsorption   $A+S \rightleftharpoons A\!\cdot\!S$

4 · Surface reaction   $A\!\cdot\!S \rightleftharpoons B\!\cdot\!S$

5 · Desorption   $B\!\cdot\!S \rightleftharpoons B+S$

Bonds form / break — chemistry. This lecture's focus.

Physical rate steps

1, 7 · External mass transfer

2, 6 · Internal (pore) mass transfer

Transport only — momentum, heat, mass. No reaction.

Chemical-rate-controlled regime: transport is fast, so the slowest chemical step sets the rate.

What is the rate expression of each chemical-rate elementary step?

Step 3 — reversible

Adsorption: $A + S \rightleftharpoons A\!\cdot\!S$

$r'_{a}=k'_a\,C_A C_S$Attachment: A meets a vacant site $S$
$r'_{d}=k'_d\,C_{A\cdot S}$De-attachment of the adsorbed pair
$r'_{ad}=k'_a\!\left(C_A C_S-\dfrac{C_{A\cdot S}}{K_A}\right),\quad K_A=\dfrac{k'_a}{k'_d}$Net rate = forward − reverse
For a gas phase, use partial pressure: $C_A \to P_A = C_A RT$.

Step 3 · from the rate law to the isotherm

Deriving the Langmuir isotherm

Reaction$A + S \rightleftharpoons A\!\cdot\!S$adsorption fast ⇒ at equilibrium
$r'_{ad}=k'_a\!\left(P_AC_S-\dfrac{C_{A\cdot S}}{K_A}\right)=0$1. the step is equilibrated, so its net rate is zero
$C_{A\cdot S}=K_AP_AC_S$2. solve for the occupied sites
$C_t=C_S+C_{A\cdot S}=C_S\left(1+K_AP_A\right)$3. site balance → substitute step 2
$\theta_A=\dfrac{C_{A\cdot S}}{C_t}=\dfrac{K_AP_A}{1+K_AP_A}$4. divide — the Langmuir isotherm

Reading the isotherm before you plot it

The two limits worth memorising

$$\theta_A=\dfrac{K_AP_A}{1+K_AP_A}\qquad C_{A\cdot S}=\dfrac{C_tK_AP_A}{1+K_AP_A}$$

Low pressure  $K_AP_A\ll1$

$\theta_A\approx K_AP_A$ — coverage proportional to pressure; the surface is nearly empty, so a rate built on $C_{A\cdot S}$ looks first order.

High pressure  $K_AP_A\gg1$

$\theta_A\to1$ — every site is taken. Adding more $A$ changes nothing, so the same rate looks zero order.

Half the sites are filled when $K_AP_A=1$, i.e. at $P_A=1/K_A$. The half-saturation pressure is the reciprocal of the adsorption constant — that is how $K_A$ is read off a plot.

Interactive 2 — surface coverage at equilibrium

Langmuir isotherm: how full is the surface?

Step 3$A + S \rightleftharpoons A\!\cdot\!S$at equilibrium ⇒ $C_{A\cdot S}=K_AP_AC_S$
$\theta_A=\dfrac{K_A P_A}{1+K_A P_A}$
Strong adsorption (large $K_A$) fills the surface at low pressure. As $P_A\to\infty$, $\theta_A\to1$ — every site occupied.
fractional coverage θ_A vs partial pressure P_A

Adsorption is exothermic ($\Delta H^{\circ}_{ads}<0$), so $K_e$ falls as $T$ rises — high temperature drives molecules off the surface.

Step 4 — three mechanisms

Surface reaction: how many sites?

Step 4$A\!\cdot\!S \rightarrow B\!\cdot\!S$  (how many sites does it use?)
MechanismElementary stepRate expression
Single site$A\!\cdot\!S \rightleftharpoons B\!\cdot\!S$$r'_S=k'_S\!\left(C_{A\cdot S}-\dfrac{C_{B\cdot S}}{K_S}\right)$
Dual site$A\!\cdot\!S + S \rightleftharpoons B\!\cdot\!S + S$$r'_S=k'_S\!\left(C_{A\cdot S}C_S-\dfrac{C_{B\cdot S}C_S}{K_S}\right)$
Eley–Rideal$A\!\cdot\!S + B(g) \rightleftharpoons C\!\cdot\!S$$r'_S=k'_S\!\left(C_{A\cdot S}P_B-\dfrac{C_{C\cdot S}}{K_S}\right)$
Eley–Rideal = an adsorbed molecule reacts with a molecule still in the gas phase.

Step 4 · dual site, three sub-cases

Dual-site reactions — and the rate law each one gives

2.1 · Same species + vacant site

$A\!\cdot\!S + S \rightleftharpoons B\!\cdot\!S + S$

$r'_S=k'_S\!\left(C_{A\cdot S}C_S-\dfrac{C_{B\cdot S}C_S}{K_S}\right)$

Adsorbed $A$ hops onto an adjacent vacant site.

2.2 · Two adsorbed reactants

$A\!\cdot\!S + B\!\cdot\!S \rightleftharpoons C\!\cdot\!S + D\!\cdot\!S$

$r'_S=k'_S\!\left(C_{A\cdot S}C_{B\cdot S}-\dfrac{C_{C\cdot S}C_{D\cdot S}}{K_S}\right)$

True Langmuir–Hinshelwood: both partners on the surface.

2.3 · Two types of site

$A\!\cdot\!S + B\!\cdot\!S^{*} \rightleftharpoons C\!\cdot\!S + S^{*}$

$r'_S=k'_S\,C_{A\cdot S}C_{B\cdot S^{*}}$

Two site balances — one per site type (e.g. Cu/ZnO).

After the site balance, each $C_S$ or $C_{i\cdot S}$ factor becomes one power of the denominator: 2.1 and 2.2 give $\left(1+\sum K_iP_i\right)^{2}$, 2.3 gives the product of two denominators, $\left(1+\sum K_iP_i\right)\left(1+\sum K_jP_j\right)$.

The bookkeeping that closes every derivation

Site balance

Steps$A+S \rightleftharpoons A\!\cdot\!S$$A\!\cdot\!S \rightleftharpoons B\!\cdot\!S$$B\!\cdot\!S \rightleftharpoons B+S$
$$C_t = C_S + \sum_i C_{i\cdot S} = C_S + C_{A\cdot S} + C_{B\cdot S} + \dots$$
$C_t$total active sites (mol / g cat) — a constant of the catalyst
$C_S$vacant (empty) sites
$C_{i\cdot S}$sites occupied by species $i$

Step 5 — reversible

Desorption: $B\!\cdot\!S \rightleftharpoons B + S$

Steps$A+S \rightleftharpoons A\!\cdot\!S$$A\!\cdot\!S \rightleftharpoons B\!\cdot\!S$$B\!\cdot\!S \rightleftharpoons B+S$
$$r'_{D}=k'_D\!\left(C_{B\cdot S}-\dfrac{C_S P_B}{K_{DB}}\right)=k'_D\!\left(C_{B\cdot S}-K_B C_S P_B\right)$$

Desorption is adsorption run backwards, so $K_B = 1/K_{DB}$.

Together with the site balance, these three rate laws are all we need to derive any solid-catalytic rate expression.

What is the rate expression when adsorption / desorption is the limiting step?

The key simplification

Chemical rate controlled: physical rate ≫ chemical rate

Reaction$\mathrm{C_6H_5CH(CH_3)_2 \rightarrow C_6H_6 + C_3H_6}\;$   $C \rightarrow B + P$
$$\text{External \& internal diffusion fast}\;\Rightarrow\; C_{Ab}=C_{As}=C_A$$

No concentration gradient — the surface "sees" the bulk concentration directly.

The observed rate now equals the rate of the slowest chemical step. Find that step, then write its rate in measurable quantities ($P$ or $C$ in the bulk).

A repeatable recipe

How to build any rate expression

Reaction$\mathrm{C_6H_5CH(CH_3)_2 \rightarrow C_6H_6 + C_3H_6}\;$   $C \rightarrow B + P$
1

Pick the rate-limiting step (RLS) — the slowest step. Every other step is assumed at equilibrium ($r=0$).

2

Apply PSSH / QSSA — surface intermediates are reactive; set their net rate to zero to eliminate $C_{i\cdot S}$.

3

Close with the site balance $\,C_t = C_S+\sum C_{i\cdot S}$, solve for $C_S$, and substitute.

Step 4 (later): compare with experimental data and regress the kinetic parameters.

Case A · adsorption controls — step by step

Adsorption RLS: the full derivation

Reaction$\mathrm{C_6H_5CH(CH_3)_2 \rightarrow C_6H_6 + C_3H_6}\;$   $C \rightarrow B + P$
Steps$C+S \rightleftharpoons C\!\cdot\!S$  (RLS)$C\!\cdot\!S \rightleftharpoons B\!\cdot\!S+P(g)$  (equil.)$B\!\cdot\!S \rightleftharpoons B+S$  (equil.)
1. Rate = the slow step$-r'_C=k'_A\!\left(P_C C_S-\tfrac{C_{C\cdot S}}{K_A}\right)$
2. Other steps at equilibrium$C_{C\cdot S}=\tfrac{C_{B\cdot S}P_P}{K_S},\;\; C_{B\cdot S}=K_B P_B C_S$
3. Site balance → solve $C_S$$C_S=\dfrac{C_t}{1+\tfrac{K_B P_B P_P}{K_S}+K_B P_B}$
4. Substitute $C_S$ back$-r'_C=\dfrac{C_t k'_A\!\left(P_C-\tfrac{P_B P_P}{K_P}\right)}{1+\tfrac{K_B P_B P_P}{K_S}+K_B P_B}$

Case C · desorption controls — step by step

Desorption RLS: the full derivation

Reaction$\mathrm{C_6H_5CH(CH_3)_2 \rightarrow C_6H_6 + C_3H_6}\;$   $C \rightarrow B + P$
Steps$C+S \rightleftharpoons C\!\cdot\!S$  (equil.)$C\!\cdot\!S \rightleftharpoons B\!\cdot\!S+P(g)$  (equil.)$B\!\cdot\!S \rightleftharpoons B+S$  (RLS)

Case C · taking the initial-rate limit

Why desorption control gives a flat initial rate

Reaction$\mathrm{C_6H_5CH(CH_3)_2 \rightarrow C_6H_6 + C_3H_6}\;$   $C \rightarrow B + P$
Steps$C+S \rightleftharpoons C\!\cdot\!S$  (equil.)$C\!\cdot\!S \rightleftharpoons B\!\cdot\!S+P(g)$  (equil.)$B\!\cdot\!S \rightleftharpoons B+S$  (RLS)
$-r'_{C}=\dfrac{C_tk'_DK_SK_C\left(\frac{P_C}{P_P}-\frac{P_B}{K_P}\right)}{1+K_CP_C+\frac{K_SK_CP_C}{P_P}}$the general result from the previous slide
$P_B\to0,\;P_P\to0 \;\Rightarrow\; \dfrac{K_SK_CP_C}{P_P}\gg 1+K_CP_C$at the reactor inlet there is no product yet, so this term runs away
$-r'_{C0}=C_tk'_D=\text{constant}$everything containing $P_{C0}$ cancels — a horizontal line
Physically: the surface is completely covered by product $B$, so the catalyst can only work as fast as $B$ leaves. Feeding more $C$ cannot help — there is nowhere to put it.

If adsorption is slow, $CO\!\cdot\!S$ is not at equilibrium with the gas — so we cannot write $C_{CO\cdot S}=K_{CO}P_{CO}C_S$ any more. What replaces it?

What is lost

The adsorption equilibrium. $K_{CO}$ no longer exists as a single constant; $k_a$ and $k_d$ must be kept separately.

What replaces it

Steady-state approximation on the intermediate: $CO\!\cdot\!S$ is formed and consumed so fast that $\dfrac{dC_{CO\cdot S}}{dt}\approx0$.

$H_2$ is Eley–Rideal here, so there is no $H\!\cdot\!S$: the site balance contains only $S$, $CO\!\cdot\!S$ and $CH_3OH\!\cdot\!S$.

Case V · step 1 — steady state on the intermediate

Four fluxes in and out of $CO\!\cdot\!S$

$$\frac{dC_{CO\cdot S}}{dt}=\underbrace{k_aP_{CO}C_S}_{\text{adsorbs}}-\underbrace{k_dC_{CO\cdot S}}_{\text{desorbs}}-\underbrace{k_{sr,f}C_{CO\cdot S}P_{H_2}^{2}}_{\text{consumed to MeOH}}+\underbrace{k_{sr,r}C_{M\cdot S}}_{\text{back from MeOH}}\approx0$$
$C_{CO\cdot S}=\dfrac{k_aP_{CO}C_S+k_{sr,r}C_{M\cdot S}}{k_d+k_{sr,f}P_{H_2}^{2}}$solve the balance for the intermediate
$C_{CO\cdot S}=\dfrac{\left(k_aP_{CO}+k_{sr,r}K_MP_M\right)C_S}{D_{sr}},\quad D_{sr}\equiv k_d+k_{sr,f}P_{H_2}^{2}$methanol desorption is equilibrated: $C_{M\cdot S}=K_MP_MC_S$
$D_{sr}$ is the total rate at which $CO\!\cdot\!S$ disappears — by desorbing back, or by reacting on. Raising $P_{H_2}$ raises $D_{sr}$, which empties the surface and frees sites.

Case V · steps 2–3 — the rate, then the site balance

Case V: the rate law

$r=r_{\text{ads,net}}=k_aP_{CO}C_S-k_dC_{CO\cdot S}$the RLS is adsorption, so the overall rate is its net rate
$r=\dfrac{C_S}{k_d+k_{sr,f}P_{H_2}^{2}}\left(k_ak_{sr,f}P_{CO}P_{H_2}^{2}-k_dk_{sr,r}K_MP_M\right)$substitute $C_{CO\cdot S}$ from the SSA and collect
$C_S=\dfrac{C_T}{1+K_MP_M+\dfrac{k_aP_{CO}+k_{sr,r}K_MP_M}{k_d+k_{sr,f}P_{H_2}^{2}}}$site balance $C_T=C_S+C_{CO\cdot S}+C_{M\cdot S}$ — $C_{CO\cdot S}$ is not dropped
$$r=\dfrac{\left(k_ak_{sr,f}P_{CO}P_{H_2}^{2}-k_dk_{sr,r}K_MP_M\right)C_T}{\left(k_d+k_{sr,f}P_{H_2}^{2}\right)\left(1+K_MP_M+\dfrac{k_aP_{CO}+k_{sr,r}K_MP_M}{k_d+k_{sr,f}P_{H_2}^{2}}\right)}$$

Case V · reading the answer

Case V: the limit that makes it recognisable

$k_{sr,f}P_{H_2}^{2}\gg k_d$ and $k_aP_{CO}\ll k_{sr,f}P_{H_2}^{2}$the surface step is so fast that $CO\!\cdot\!S$ never accumulates
$$r\approx\dfrac{k_aP_{CO}\,C_T}{1+K_MP_M}$$

$P_{CO}$ is not in the denominator

Order 1 in CO with no saturation — the classic adsorption-limited signature from Lecture 3-3.

$H_2$ disappears

Order in $H_2$ → 0: hydrogen only helps to clear the surface, it does not set the rate.

Methanol still inhibits

Through $K_MP_M$ in the denominator — and through the back-reaction term in the full expression.

Problem V & VI · how the answer is assembled

Two site balances + QSSA close the problem

1. Equilibrate steps 1, 2, 5, 6$C_{A\cdot S}=K_1C_AC_S,\;\; C_{O\cdot S^{*}}=\sqrt{K_2C_{O_2}}\,C_{S^{*}}$
2. QSSA on the intermediate$R_{B\cdot S}=k_1C_{A\cdot S}C_{O\cdot S^{*}}-k_2C_{B\cdot S}C_{O\cdot S^{*}}=0\Rightarrow C_{B\cdot S}=\dfrac{k_1}{k_2}C_{A\cdot S}$
3. Site balances — note that $C_{B\cdot S}$ is not independent$C_t=C_S\Big(1+K_1C_A+\underbrace{\tfrac{k_1}{k_2}K_1C_A}_{\text{this term is }C_{B\cdot S}}+K_5C_C\Big)$  ·  $C_t^{*}=C_{S^{*}}\left(1+\sqrt{K_2C_{O_2}}+K_6C_{H_2O}\right)$

The five things to remember

Key Takeaways

1

Separate the seven steps: only adsorption, surface reaction, desorption are chemical. Prove chemical control before using these rate laws.

2

Build every rate law the same way: pick the RLS → equilibrate the rest → PSSH → site balance → regress.

3

Read the initial-rate curve first: linear = adsorption, saturating = surface reaction, flat = desorption.

4

Decode the LH–HW form: denominator lists adsorbed species, its power counts sites, a maximum means dual site, a $\sqrt{P}$ means dissociation.

5

Multiply by activity $a(t)$ when the catalyst ages, and get the decay order from the linearized plot.
