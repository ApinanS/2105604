Why adsorption & desorption count as chemical steps

It is chemisorption — real bonds form

Steps 3–5 were called chemical. Sticking to a surface does not sound like a reaction, so that claim needs a reason.

  • Physisorption — weak van der Waals attraction. No bond, no reaction.
  • Chemisorption — a real bond to the site, so it obeys a chemical rate law.
  • Example: ethylidene chemisorbed on platinum.

Step 3 — reversible

Adsorption: $A + S \rightleftharpoons A\!\cdot\!S$

$r'_{a}=k'_a\,C_A C_S$Attachment: A meets a vacant site $S$
$r'_{d}=k'_d\,C_{A\cdot S}$De-attachment of the adsorbed pair
$r'_{ad}=k'_a\!\left(C_A C_S-\dfrac{C_{A\cdot S}}{K_A}\right),\quad K_A=\dfrac{k'_a}{k'_d}$Net rate = forward − reverse
For a gas phase, use partial pressure: $C_A \to P_A = C_A RT$.

Step 3 · from the rate law to the isotherm

Deriving the Langmuir isotherm

Reaction$A + S \rightleftharpoons A\!\cdot\!S$adsorption fast ⇒ at equilibrium
$r'_{ad}=k'_a\!\left(P_AC_S-\dfrac{C_{A\cdot S}}{K_A}\right)=0$1. the step is equilibrated, so its net rate is zero
$C_{A\cdot S}=K_AP_AC_S$2. solve for the occupied sites
$C_t=C_S+C_{A\cdot S}=C_S\left(1+K_AP_A\right)$3. site balance → substitute step 2
$\theta_A=\dfrac{C_{A\cdot S}}{C_t}=\dfrac{K_AP_A}{1+K_AP_A}$4. divide — the Langmuir isotherm

Reading the isotherm before you plot it

The two limits worth memorising

From the previous slide$\theta_A=\dfrac{K_AP_A}{1+K_AP_A}\qquad C_{A\cdot S}=\dfrac{C_tK_AP_A}{1+K_AP_A}$

Low pressure  $K_AP_A\ll1$

$\theta_A\approx K_AP_A$ — the surface is nearly empty, so coverage tracks pressure and any rate built on $C_{A\cdot S}$ looks first order.

High pressure  $K_AP_A\gg1$

$\theta_A\to1$ — every site is taken. More $A$ changes nothing, so the same rate looks zero order.

Langmuir isotherm: how full is the surface?

Step 3$A + S \rightleftharpoons A\!\cdot\!S$at equilibrium ⇒ $C_{A\cdot S}=K_AP_AC_S$

Large $K_A$ fills the surface at low pressure; as $P_A\to\infty$, $\theta_A\to1$.

A strongly adsorbing species saturates early, so the rate stops responding to its pressure.

Interactive 3 — adsorption weakens as it gets hotter

Equilibrium constant vs temperature (exothermic adsorption)

Step 3$A + S \rightleftharpoons A\!\cdot\!S$exothermic: $\Delta H^{\circ}_{ads}<0$
$K_e(T)=K_e(T_1)\,e^{\frac{\Delta H^{\circ}_{ads}}{R}\left(\frac{1}{T_1}-\frac{1}{T}\right)}$
Adsorption is exothermic ($\Delta H^{\circ}_{ads}<0$), so $K_e$ falls as $T$ rises — high temperature drives molecules off the surface.

Heating a catalyst empties its surface, so raising $T$ can lower a rate that depends on coverage.

Step 4 · dual site, three sub-cases

Three ways two sites get usedstep 1 of 2

2.1 · Species + vacant site

$A\!\cdot\!S + S \rightleftharpoons B\!\cdot\!S + S$

$r'_S=k'_S\!\left(C_{A\cdot S}C_S-\dfrac{C_{B\cdot S}C_S}{K_S}\right)$

Adsorbed $A$ hops onto an adjacent vacant site.

2.2 · Two adsorbed reactants

$A\!\cdot\!S + B\!\cdot\!S \rightleftharpoons C\!\cdot\!S + D\!\cdot\!S$

$r'_S=k'_S\!\left(C_{A\cdot S}C_{B\cdot S}-\dfrac{C_{C\cdot S}C_{D\cdot S}}{K_S}\right)$

True Langmuir–Hinshelwood: both partners on the surface.

2.3 · Two types of site

$A\!\cdot\!S + B\!\cdot\!S^{*} \rightleftharpoons C\!\cdot\!S + S^{*}$

$r'_S=k'_S\,C_{A\cdot S}C_{B\cdot S^{*}}$

Two site balances — one per site type (e.g. Cu/ZnO).

Step 4 · what the shape costs you later

Each site factor becomes one powerstep 2 of 2

The three shapes look alike now. They separate once the site balance replaces every $C_S$ and $C_{i\cdot S}$.

The substitution to come$C_S=\dfrac{C_t}{1+\sum_i K_iP_i}$
Sub-caseSite factorsDenominator it produces
2.1 & 2.2two of the same type$\left(1+\sum K_iP_i\right)^{2}$
2.3one of each type$\left(1+\sum K_iP_i\right)\!\left(1+\sum K_jP_j\right)$

The exponent on the denominator is a fingerprint of how many sites the slow step needs.

The bookkeeping that closes every derivation

Site balance

Steps$A+S \rightleftharpoons A\!\cdot\!S$$A\!\cdot\!S \rightleftharpoons B\!\cdot\!S$$B\!\cdot\!S \rightleftharpoons B+S$
$$C_t = C_S + \sum_i C_{i\cdot S} = C_S + C_{A\cdot S} + C_{B\cdot S} + \dots$$
$C_t$total active sites (mol / g cat) — a constant of the catalyst
$C_S$vacant (empty) sites
$C_{i\cdot S}$sites occupied by species $i$

Step 5 — reversible

Desorption: $B\!\cdot\!S \rightleftharpoons B + S$

Steps$A+S \rightleftharpoons A\!\cdot\!S$$A\!\cdot\!S \rightleftharpoons B\!\cdot\!S$$B\!\cdot\!S \rightleftharpoons B+S$
$$r'_{D}=k'_D\!\left(C_{B\cdot S}-\dfrac{C_S P_B}{K_{DB}}\right)=k'_D\!\left(C_{B\cdot S}-K_B C_S P_B\right)$$

Desorption is adsorption run backwards, so $K_B = 1/K_{DB}$.

Together with the site balance, these three rate laws are all we need to derive any solid-catalytic rate expression.

The key simplification

Chemical rate controlled: physical rate ≫ chemical rate

Reaction$\mathrm{C_6H_5CH(CH_3)_2 \rightarrow C_6H_6 + C_3H_6}\;$   $C \rightarrow B + P$
$$\text{External \& internal diffusion fast}\;\Rightarrow\; C_{Ab}=C_{As}=C_A$$

No concentration gradient — the surface "sees" the bulk concentration directly.

The observed rate now equals the rate of the slowest chemical step. Find that step, then write its rate in measurable quantities ($P$ or $C$ in the bulk).

A repeatable recipe

How to build any rate expressionstep 1 of 2

Reaction$\mathrm{C_6H_5CH(CH_3)_2 \rightarrow C_6H_6 + C_3H_6}\;$   $C \rightarrow B + P$
1

Pick the rate-limiting step (RLS). Every other step is then taken as equilibrated.

2

Apply the pseudo-steady-state hypothesis (PSSH) to eliminate the surface intermediates.

3

Close with the site balance, solve for $C_S$, and substitute it back.

The reason each move is legal

What each step buysstep 2 of 2

Three moves, three reasons. Skip any one and the rate law still has an unmeasurable site concentration in it.

RLS is slowa fast step stays at equilibrium, so its net rate is zero
