Classification

Four ideal reactors, four sets of assumptions

Batch

No flow in or out · well mixed · constant $V$ · unsteady.

Semi-batch

Inlet or outlet, not both · well mixed · $V$ grows · unsteady.

Stirred tank (CSTR)

Continuous stirred-tank reactor: both streams · perfectly mixed · steady.

Tubular (PFR)

Plug-flow reactor: both streams · no radial gradient · steady.

Each has a solid-catalytic twin: normalize the rate per unit volume ($-r_A$) or per kilogram of catalyst ($-r'_A$).

The master equation

General mole balance on species $j$

$$\underbrace{F_{j0}-F_j}_{\text{in}-\text{out}}+\underbrace{\int_V \sum_{i=1}^{R}\nu_{ij}\,r_i\,dV}_{\text{generation}}=\underbrace{\frac{dN_j}{dt}}_{\text{accumulation}}$$
$F_{j0},\,F_j$

mol/s
$\nu_{ij}$
stoichiometric coefficient of $j$ in reaction $i$ — negative if consumed
–
$r_i$
rate of reaction $i$ per unit volume, one of $R$ reactions
mol/(m³·s)
$N_j$

mol
$V$

The balance needs $\sum_i\nu_{ij}r_i$. With two reactions running, that sum is where students lose the sign.

Rate & stoichiometry

One species, several reactions

$$\text{A}+2\text{B}\to 4\text{C}+\text{D}\quad r_1=k_1 C_A C_B^2$$ $$2\text{A}+\text{B}\to \text{U}+3\text{G}\quad r_2=k_2 C_A^2 C_B$$
$$r_A=\nu_{1A}r_1+\nu_{2A}r_2=-r_1-2r_2$$
Reading the signs
  • $\nu_{ij}<0$ → consumed; $\nu_{ij}>0$ → generated
  • A carries $-1$ in reaction 1 and $-2$ in reaction 2, so both terms subtract
  • The subscript $1$ in $r_1$ counts the reaction, never the species

Temperature dependence

Arrhenius law, written the way the solver wants it

Rate constant

$$k(T)=Ae^{-E/RT}=k(T_0)\,e^{\frac{E}{R}\left(\frac{1}{T_0}-\frac1T\right)}$$

Equilibrium constant

$$K_e(T)=K_e(T_1)\,e^{\frac{\Delta H_{rxn}^0}{R}\left(\frac{1}{T_1}-\frac1T\right)}$$

$T_0$

Lecture 5-1

What section 5-1 settled

  • There is one mole balance. A reactor is a set of assumptions that deletes terms from it.
  • Signs come from $\nu_{ij}$ alone: negative consumes, positive generates, and every reaction contributes.

Design equation

Batch reactor: homogeneous and solid-catalytic

General balance, section 5-1, with both flow terms zero$\displaystyle\int_V\sum_i\nu_{ij}r_i\,dV=\frac{dN_j}{dt}$
Homogeneous, constant $V$
$$\frac{1}{V}\frac{dN_j}{dt}=\sum_i \nu_{ij}r_i\;\Rightarrow\;\frac{dC_j}{dt}=\sum_i \nu_{ij}r_i$$
Solid-catalytic, rate per kg catalyst
$$\frac{dN_j}{dt}=\sum_i \nu_{ij}r'_i\,W_{cat},\qquad C_j=\frac{N_j}{V}$$

Only a constant $V$ may cross the derivative. In 5-3 it moves, and this step dies.

One balance per species is easy. Keeping six of them consistent is what a solver is for.

Worked example · liquid-phase multiple reaction

Two reactions, one of them reversible

$$r_1=k_1C_AC_B^2-k_2C_CC_D^3,\qquad r_2=k_3C_A^2C_C$$
Find the concentration profile of every component.

The equations this example needs

Six mole balances, constant $V$
$$\begin{aligned} \tfrac{dC_A}{dt}&=-r_1-2r_2 & \tfrac{dC_B}{dt}&=-2r_1\\ \tfrac{dC_C}{dt}&=r_1-r_2 & \tfrac{dC_D}{dt}&=3r_1\\ \tfrac{dC_E}{dt}&=r_2 & \tfrac{dC_F}{dt}&=r_2 \end{aligned}$$

Eleven relations for eleven unknowns: six $C_j$, two rates, three $k_i$. Count before you type.

Lecture 5-2

What section 5-2 settled

  • Constant volume is the whole trick: it turns a mole balance into $dC_j/dt=\sum_i\nu_{ij}r_i$.
  • Write one balance per species, add the rate laws and the property relations, then integrate.
  • Batch time is read off the profile, not solved for — you integrate until $X$ is where you want it.
  • Temperature is never a free lever: it moves conversion and selectivity in opposite directions.

Design equation · variable volume

Add a feed term, and an equation for $V$

General balance, section 5-1, with no outlet stream$F_{j0}+\displaystyle\int_V\sum_i\nu_{ij}r_i\,dV=\frac{dN_j}{dt}$
$$\frac{dN_j}{dt}=F_{j0}+\Big(\textstyle\sum_i \nu_{ij}r_i\Big)V,\qquad \frac{dV}{dt}=v_0$$
$F_{j0}$molar feed of $j$ — zero for every species not fed
$\left(\sum_i\nu_{ij}r_i\right)V$generation uses the current volume, not $V_0$
$C_j=N_j/V$concentration now falls by reaction and by dilution

$v_0$ = volumetric feed rate, m³/s; $V_0$ = the initial charge, m³.

Same chemistry as 5-2, one operating change: A arrives gradually instead of all at once.

Design equation · steady state

No accumulation leaves an algebraic balance

General balance, section 5-1, steady and perfectly mixed$F_{j0}-F_j+\big(\textstyle\sum_i\nu_{ij}r_i\big)V=0$
$$\frac{F_j-F_{j0}}{V}=\sum_i \nu_{ij}r_i\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad V=\frac{F_{j0}-F_j}{-\sum_i \nu_{ij}r_i}$$

$v$ = outlet volumetric flow, m³/s; $\tau$ = space time, s.

Same two reactions once more, now run continuously. The unknown is no longer a time — it is a volume.

In a liquid the volumetric flow is a constant you can divide by. In a gas it is another unknown.

Why gas reactors are different

Volumetric flow moves with moles, pressure and temperature

Liquid phase
$$v=v_0\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;C_j=\frac{F_j}{v_0}$$
Gas phase, ideal
$$v=v_0\left(\frac{P_0}{P}\right)\!\left(\frac{T}{T_0}\right)\!\left(\frac{F_T}{F_{T0}}\right)$$

$F_T=\sum_j F_j$ is the total molar flow; $F_{T0}$ is its inlet value.

Both reactions here make more moles than they consume, so $F_T$ rises, the stream expands, every concentration is diluted, and the tube must be longer than the liquid case.

  • Under strong pore diffusion the measured order becomes $(n+1)/2$ — a real experimental trap.
  • $\eta$ must be recomputed inside the integration whenever the true order is not one.
  • Pellet size is the one variable that moves pore diffusion, pressure drop and bed length at once.

3

For gas beds always carry volume change and pressure drop — either can dominate the design.

Validate every numerical answer against a closed form or a criterion you can compute by hand.
