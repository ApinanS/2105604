The master equations

Mole balance and energy balance, solved together

Mole balance on species $i$

$$\frac{dN_i}{dt}=F_{i0}-F_i+\sum_j \nu_{ij}\,r_j\,V$$

Energy balance on the contents

$$\Big(\textstyle\sum_i N_iC_{p,i}\Big)\frac{dT}{dt}=\dot Q+\dot W_s-\sum_j r_jV\Delta H_{Rxj}-\sum_i F_{i0}C_{p,i}(T-T_F)$$
$N_i,\,F_{i0},\,F_i$
moles held; molar flow in and out of species $i$
mol, mol/s
$\nu_{ij},\,r_j$
coefficient of $i$ in reaction $j$, and that reaction's rate
–, mol/(m³·s)
$C_{p,i}$
molar heat capacity — the thermal inertia each mole carries
J/(mol·K)
$\dot Q,\,\dot W_s$
heat through the wall and shaft work into the fluid
W
$\Delta H_{Rxj},\,T_F$
heat of reaction $j$; temperature the feed arrives at
J/mol, °C

In Lecture 5 the temperature was given, so the mole balance stood alone. Now it does not.

The coupling

Temperature is inside every rate constant

$$k_j(T)=k_{j0}\exp\!\left[\frac{E_j}{R}\left(\frac{1}{T_0}-\frac{1}{T}\right)\right]$$

Rates depend on $T$; $T$ depends on the heat those rates release.

Solve the mole balance alone and you must already know $T(t)$ — which the energy balance has not produced yet.
So the two sets of ODEs are integrated as one system, one time step at a time. That is the whole method of this lecture.

Batch: no stream in, no stream out

What a batch leaves of the mole balance

$$\frac{dN_i}{dt}=\Big(\sum_j \nu_{ij}\,r_j\Big)V$$

Constant $V$ moves inside the derivative

$$\frac{dC_i}{dt}=\sum_j \nu_{ij}\,r_j$$
The reaction system used all lecture$\text{A}+2\text{B}\rightleftharpoons\text{C}+3\text{D}$;  $2\text{A}+\text{C}\to\text{E}+\text{F}$
$$r_1=k_1C_AC_B^2-k_2C_CC_D^3,\qquad r_2=k_3C_A^2C_C$$

A is eaten by both routes: $\nu_{1A}=-1$ and $\nu_{2A}=-2$.

Batch: the feed term dies too

Building the batch energy balance, term by term

$$\Big(\textstyle\sum_i N_iC_{p,i}\Big)\frac{dT}{dt}=\dot Q+\dot W_s-\sum_j r_jV\,\Delta H_{Rxj}(T)$$
$\sum_i F_{i0}C_{p,i}(T-T_F)=0$nothing is fed, so nothing must be warmed
$\Delta H_{Rxj}(T)=\Delta H^{\circ}_{Rxj}+\Delta C_{p,j}(T-T_R)$the tabulated heat is quoted at $T_R$, not at $T$
$\Delta C_{p,j}=\sum_i \nu_{ij}C_{p,i}$same coefficients as the mole balance, applied to $C_p$

Left side is thermal inertia; right side is heat in minus heat consumed by reaction.

The two non-reaction terms

Where $\dot Q$ and $\dot W_s$ come from

Jacket — heat leaving through the wall

$$\dot Q=UA\,\Delta T_{LM}=\dot m_{cw}C_{p,cw}(T_{cw,in}-T_{cw,out})$$
$$\Delta T_{LM}=\frac{(T_{cw,in}-T)-(T_{cw,out}-T)}{\ln\!\big[(T_{cw,in}-T)/(T_{cw,out}-T)\big]}$$

Writing $\dot Q$ twice gives both the area $A$ and the coolant flow.

Impeller — work that becomes heat

$$\dot W_s=N_p\,\rho\,N^3D^5,\qquad Re=\frac{\rho D^2N}{\mu}$$
Impeller$N_p$ at $Re>10^4$
Propeller, 3 blade0.35
Pitched-blade turbine 4×45°1.27
Rushton, 6-blade disc5.0

$N_p$ is constant only once the flow is turbulent — check $Re$ before you trust it.

Lecture 7-1

What section 7-1 settled

  • Unsteady non-isothermal design is one system: mole balances and one energy balance, marched together.
  • A batch kills the flow terms; what is left is generation against thermal inertia $\sum N_iC_{p,i}$.
  • Correct $\Delta H_{Rx}$ from $T_R$ to $T$, and write $\dot Q$ twice to get the area and the coolant flow.
  • Cooling governs the peak; the extent of reaction is decided in the first instants, while it is hot.

A batch is closed. Open one inlet and two terms wake up at once.

One inlet, no outlet

Moles and volume, both integrated

A is fed; the rest only react

$$\frac{dN_A}{dt}=F_{A0}+\big(\nu_{1A}r_1+\nu_{2A}r_2\big)V$$
$$\frac{dN_i}{dt}=\Big(\sum_j\nu_{ij}r_j\Big)V\qquad(i\neq A)$$

The volume is a state, not a constant

$$\frac{dV}{dt}=v_0,\qquad V(0)=V_0$$
$V$ is inside the derivative, so $\dfrac{1}{V}\dfrac{dN_i}{dt}\neq\dfrac{dC_i}{dt}$ any more. Keep the balance in moles.

Convert with $C_i=N_i/V$ only after both $N_i$ and $V$ have been advanced.

The term a batch did not have

Paying to heat the feed

$$\Big(\textstyle\sum_i N_iC_{p,i}\Big)\frac{dT}{dt}=\dot Q-\sum_j r_jV\Delta H_{Rxj}-\sum_i F_{i0}C_{p,i}(T-T_F)$$
$-\sum_i F_{i0}C_{p,i}(T-T_F)$sensible heat to lift the feed from $T_F$ to $T$
$\dot Q=UA\,(T_{cool}-T)$lumped jacket; negative once the vessel is hotter
$\dot W_s\approx0$shaft work is negligible beside this reaction heat

A cold feed is free cooling — but only while there is feed still arriving.

Same chemistry as 7-1, same kinetics. Only the way the reactants meet has changed.

The equations this example needs

Six balances in moles, plus the volume
$$\begin{aligned} \tfrac{dN_A}{dt}&=F_{A0}+(-r_1-2r_2)V & \tfrac{dN_B}{dt}&=-2r_1V\\ \tfrac{dN_C}{dt}&=(r_1-r_2)V & \tfrac{dN_D}{dt}&=3r_1V\\ \tfrac{dN_E}{dt}&=r_2V & \tfrac{dN_F}{dt}&=r_2V \end{aligned}$$
$$\frac{dV}{dt}=v_0,\qquad C_i=\frac{N_i}{V}$$
One energy balance, with the feed term
$$\Big(\textstyle\sum_i N_iC_{p,i}\Big)\tfrac{dT}{dt}=\dot Q-\textstyle\sum_j r_jV\Delta H_{Rxj}-F_{A0}C_{p,A}(T-T_F)$$
$$r_1=k_1C_AC_B^2-k_2C_CC_D^3,\quad r_2=k_3C_A^2C_C$$

Eight states now: six $N_i$, the volume $V$, and the temperature $T$. The jacket is $\dot Q=UA(T_{cool}-T)$.

Lecture 7-2

What section 7-2 settled

  • An inlet adds two things: an $F_{i0}$ term in one balance, and $V$ as a state of its own.
  • With $V$ moving, the balance stays in moles; concentrations are recovered afterwards.
  • The energy balance gains $-\sum F_{i0}C_{p,i}(T-T_F)$ — cold feed absorbs heat as it arrives.
  • Feed rate is a heat-removal handle: it decides how fast the reaction heat is released.

Set up first, solve second · the material side

Normalising the rate to catalyst mass

The step that makes scale-up possible
$$V_Lk_1=m_{cat,L}\,k_{s1,0}\;\Rightarrow\;k_{s1,0}=\frac{V_Lk_1}{m_{cat,L}}$$

$k_{s,0}$ is in L/(min·g$_{cat}$): a property of the catalyst, not of the vessel.

Then the ordinary balances
$$\frac{1}{m_{cat}}\frac{dN_i}{dt}=\sum_j\nu_{ij}r_{sj},\qquad m_{cat}=0.03\,\rho_{sol}V$$
$$k_{sj}=k_{sj,0}\exp\!\left[\frac{E_j}{R}\left(\frac{1}{T_{lab}}-\frac{1}{T}\right)\right]$$

The pilot holds six times the lab catalyst, so the same chemistry runs six times faster.

Q1 asks for a temperature profile, so one more state joins the six: $T$ itself.

Problem III · the engineering verdict

What taking the product out buys, and what it costs

In favour

  • Product. 113.9 mol recovered against 0 — reaction 2 destroys all of it in a sealed vessel.
  • Safety. The excursion drops 125.2 → 96.6 °C on the same jacket.
  • Utility. 30 % less heat to remove, 837 → 590 kg of cooling water.
  • Purity. It leaves as a vapour, so the separation is already half done.

Against

  • Capex. A condenser and a receiver, plus the 3.6 MJ of duty they carry.
  • Level. The liquid falls 13.5 L, 8.4 % — wetted jacket area falls with it.
  • Self-limiting. Below C’s 75 °C boiling point the line simply stops.
  • Control. The take-off follows $r_1$, so an operator cannot set it directly.

Worth it here — but neither reactor is safe until the jacket is sized for 463 kW.

Lecture 7

Key Takeaways

1

Solve the mole and energy balances as one system — temperature sits inside every rate constant.

2

Let the reactor delete the terms: no flow for a batch, one inlet and a moving volume for a semi-batch, both streams for a continuous stirred tank.

3

Compare the thermal mass with the duty before you integrate — a jacket can own a profile the chemistry never touches.

4

Heats of reaction move with temperature: $\Delta H_{Rx}(T)=\Delta H^{\circ}_{Rx}+\Delta C_p(T-T_R)$, and at a 169 °C peak that correction is most of the number.

5

An explicit integrator does not warn you. Halve the step and re-run — if the answer moves, the step was the answer.
