What is the heat of reaction, and how does it change with temperature?

Every non-isothermal design starts here: the reaction's own heat release is the source term that will move the reactor temperature.

Definition

Heat of reaction from heats of formation

$$\Delta H_{Rx}^{\circ}(T_R)=\sum_i \nu_i\, \Delta H_{f,i}^{\circ}(T_R),\qquad T_R=25^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$$
$\Delta H_{Rx}<0$exothermic — releases heat, so $T$ rises
$\Delta H_{Rx}>0$endothermic — absorbs heat, so $T$ falls
$\Delta H_f^{\circ}$ is the heat to form one mole of a compound from its elements at 25 °C and 1 bar, tabulated per phase. Pure elements are zero by definition.
Basis: one mole of the species whose coefficient you divided by — state it, or the number means nothing.

Temperature correction

From 25 °C to the reaction temperature

$$\Delta H_{Rx}(T)=\Delta H_{Rx}^{\circ}(T_R)+\underbrace{\int_{T_R}^{T}\Delta C_p\,dT}_{\text{sensible-heat correction}}$$
$$\Delta C_p=\sum_i \nu_i\,C_{p,i},\qquad C_{p,i}=a_i+b_iT+c_iT^2+d_iT^3$$

Constant $C_p$ makes the correction linear: $\Delta H_{Rx}(T)\approx\Delta H_{Rx}^{\circ}+\Delta C_p(T-T_R)$.

The $a_i,b_i,c_i,d_i$ are the polynomial coefficients printed in property tables — the only place this data comes from.

Getting to the reaction state

Account for phase changes on the way

$$H_i(T)=\Delta H_{f,i}^{\circ}+\!\int_{T_R}^{T_b}\!C_p^{L}\,dT+\Delta H_{vap}+\!\int_{T_b}^{T}\!C_p^{G}\,dT$$

$T_b$ = normal boiling point (K), $\Delta H_{vap}$ = latent heat (kJ/mol), $C_p^L,C_p^G$ = liquid and gas heat capacities.

Fed as liquid but reacting as vapour? Add $\Delta H_{vap}$ at $T_b$ — it often dwarfs both sensible terms.
Liquid → Liquidsensible heat only
Liquid → Gassensible $+\;\Delta H_{vap}$
Gas → Gassensible heat only

  2. The same $\nu_i$ build $\Delta H_{Rx}^{\circ}$ and $\Delta C_p$ — write them once, use them twice.

Section 6-1 · summary

Heat of reaction — what to remember

  • $\Delta H_{Rx}^{\circ}=\sum_i\nu_i\Delta H_{f,i}^{\circ}$ at 25 °C; the sign names exothermic ($<0$) or endothermic ($>0$).
  • Correct to $T$ with $\int_{T_R}^{T}\Delta C_p\,dT$ — it tilts the value by a few kJ/mol and rarely changes the sign.

  • Always state the basis, and back-fit $E$ from two $k$ measurements before trusting any Arrhenius line.

Section 6-2 · summary

Energy balance — what to remember

  • Working form: $\dot Q-\dot W_s-\sum_j r_jV\Delta H_{Rxj}=\sum_i F_{i0}C_{p,i}(T-T_0)$ — reaction heat on the left, sensible heat on the right.
  • Adiabatic operation changes nothing but one term: $\dot Q=0$, and the released heat has only the stream to go into, $-\sum_j r_jV\Delta H_{Rxj}=\sum_i F_{i0}C_{p,i}(T-T_0)$.
  • $\dot Q=UA\,\Delta T_{LM}$ is the design handle; $\dot W_s=N_p\rho N^3D^5$ is usually a small correction.
  • Unsteady operation adds $\big(\sum_iN_iC_{p,i}\big)dT/dt$ — the term that lets a start-up overshoot.

Two coupled balances

CSTR mole & energy balance

Mole balance, each species

$$0=F_{i0}-F_i+\Big(\textstyle\sum_j \nu_{ij}r_j\Big)V$$

Energy balance, steady state

$$\dot Q+\dot W_s-\!\sum_j r_jV\Delta H_{Rxj}=\!\sum_i F_{i0}C_{p,i}(T-T_0)$$
Coupled through $T$: every $k_j(T)$ sits in the mole balance, and the heat those same $r_j$ release sits in the energy balance.
A set of nonlinear algebraic equations — no analytical solution, so we use a simultaneous solver.
Solve them together, or not at all.

The first source term · where $\dot Q$ comes from

Heat rate: $\dot Q=UA\,(LMTD)$

$$\dot Q=UA\,(LMTD)$$

$U$ = overall coefficient W/(m²·K), $A$ = jacket area m², $LMTD$ = log-mean temperature difference K.

From surrounding to system: every difference is $T_{media}-T$, so $\dot Q>0$ heats and $\dot Q<0$ cools.
$$LMTD=\frac{\Delta T_1-\Delta T_2}{\ln(\Delta T_1/\Delta T_2)}$$
$$\dot Q=\dot m_{media}\,C_{p,media}\,(T_{out}-T_{in})$$

The transient path to steady state

Case IV — start-up, and why it overshoots

$$\dot Q+\dot W_s-\sum_j r_jV\Delta H_{Rxj}=\Big(\sum_i N_iC_{p,i}\Big)\frac{dT}{dt}+\sum_i F_{i0}C_{p,i}(T-T_0)$$
$N_i$ is the number of moles of $i$ held in the tank, not a flow — the thermal inertia of the charge.
Early in start-up the charge is at full concentration while the jacket has barely begun to work, so $T$ climbs past its steady value before settling — the key commissioning check.

Section 6-3 · summary

Non-isothermal CSTR — what to remember

  • Both balances are algebraic and coupled through $T$: solve them simultaneously, never in sequence.
  • Given both coolant temperatures, the jacket returns $\dot m_{CW}$ — and $UA\gg\sum F_{j0}C_{p,j}$ pins the tank to the water.
  • One tank, four modes: adiabatic 251 °C, the 21.4 m² jacket 43.1 °C, isothermal 50 °C, start-up peaking at 124 °C.
  • The adiabatic tank converts the most A and makes the least C — read $F_C$, not $X_A$.
  • $G(T)=R(T)$ can have three roots: the outer two are stable, and extinction is lost tangency.

Now the balances are differential in volume

PFR mole & energy balance

Mole balance

$$\frac{dF_i}{dV}=\sum_j \nu_{ij}\,r_j$$

Energy balance

$$\Big(\textstyle\sum_i F_iC_{p,i}\Big)\frac{dT}{dV}=-\sum_j r_j\Delta H_{Rxj}+\dot Q_a$$
A slice exchanges no mass with its neighbours, so it behaves exactly like a small batch reactor drifting downstream — residence time replaced by position.
Gas phase$v$ changes with $T$, $P$ and total moles
Liquid phase$v\approx v_0$, density essentially constant

Concentration depends on the phase

Gas- versus liquid-phase concentrations

Liquid phaseGas phase
Volumetric flow$v\approx v_0$$v=v_0\dfrac{P_0}{P}\dfrac{T}{T_0}\dfrac{F_T}{F_{T0}}$
Concentration$C_i=F_i/v_0$$C_i=F_i/v$
As $T$ riseslittle changegas expands, so $C_i$ falls
Expansion is a built-in brake: hotter gas is more dilute, so the rate limits itself. Everything else fixed, the gas run is the milder one.

Wall heat exchange

The distributed cooling term $\dot Q_a$

$$\dot Q_a=Ua\,(T_a-T),\qquad a=\frac{4}{D}$$

$a$ is heat-transfer area per unit reactor volume, m¹⁻: for a tube it is wall area $\pi DL$ over volume $\tfrac{\pi}{4}D^2L$.

$\dot Q_a>0$ heats the tube when $T_a>T$; $\dot Q_a<0$ cools it. Setting $Ua=0$ recovers the adiabatic reactor exactly.
Narrow tubes cool well because $a\propto1/D$ — hence bundles of thin tubes, not one wide pipe.

Differential energy balance

Energy balance across one slice $dV$steps 1–3

heat generated $+$ heat exchanged $=$ enthalpy riseword balance on a slice of volume $dV$
$-\sum_j r_j\Delta H_{Rxj}\,dV+\dot Q_a\,dV=\Big(\sum_i F_iC_{p,i}\Big)dT$write each term over that slice
$\dfrac{dT}{dV}=\dfrac{-\sum_j r_j\Delta H_{Rxj}+Ua(T_a-T)}{\sum_i F_iC_{p,i}}$divide by $dV$: the ODE we integrate

Valid while the slice is well mixed radially — not for a wide, strongly cooled tube.

Section 6-4 · summary

Non-isothermal PFR — what to remember

  • $dF_i/dV=\sum_j\nu_{ij}r_j$ and $\big(\sum_iF_iC_{p,i}\big)dT/dV=-\sum_jr_j\Delta H_{Rxj}+\dot Q_a$, integrated together.
  • Gas-phase expansion dilutes the feed as the tube heats, damping the runaway a liquid system cannot damp.
  • $\dot Q_a=Ua(T_a-T)$ with $a=4/D$: thin tubes cool well, and $Ua=0$ recovers the adiabatic case.
  • Cooling creates a hot spot; too much of it quenches the inlet and the reaction never lights off.

Catalyst weight is the independent variable

Mole balance and the bed properties behind it

$$\frac{dF_i}{dW}=\sum_j \nu_{ij}\,r'_j$$
$\phi=V_{void}/V_{bed}$void fraction of the packing
$\rho_b=(1-\phi)\rho_c$bulk density from particle density
$W=\rho_b\,A_c\,z$catalyst weight from bed length $z$
$r'_j$ is the rate per kilogram of catalyst — the natural basis for a solid-catalysed reaction, and the reason $W$ replaces $V$.
A porous pellet has more area but lower $\rho_c$; $\phi$ and $\rho_c$ together fix how much catalyst a bed volume holds.

Section 6-5 · the same balance, now per kilogram of catalyst

Fixed-bed reactor — energy balance

$$\sum_{j=1}^{N}F_jC_{p,j}\,\frac{dT}{dW}=\frac{d\dot Q}{dW}-\frac{d\dot W_s}{dW}-\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{M}r'_i\,\Delta H_{r i}\Big)$$
$\dfrac{d\dot W_s}{dW}=0\;\Rightarrow\;\sum_j F_jC_{p,j}\dfrac{dT}{dW}=\dfrac{d\dot Q}{dW}-\sum_i r'_i\Delta H_{r i}$nothing turns in a packed tube, so shaft work is gone

Pressure drop matters for gas beds

The Ergun equation, and the parameter $\alpha$

$$\frac{dP}{dz}=-\frac{G}{\rho\,d_p}\Big(\frac{1-\phi}{\phi^3}\Big)\Big[\frac{150(1-\phi)\mu}{d_p}+1.75\,G\Big]$$
$G=\rho u$

Falling $P$ lowers every gas concentration, and with it the rate.

$y=\dfrac{P}{P_0},\qquad\dfrac{dy}{dW}=-\dfrac{\alpha}{2y}\,\dfrac{T}{T_0}\,\dfrac{F_T}{F_{T0}}$divided by $\rho_bA_c$, so it runs on catalyst weight
$\alpha=\dfrac{2\beta_0}{A_c\,\rho_b\,P_0},\qquad\beta_0=-\dfrac{dP}{dz}\Big|_{0}$$\beta_0$ is the Ergun right-hand side at the feed — one number
This bed: $\beta_0=5.9\times10^{4}$ Pa/m, so $\alpha=0.0058$ kg$^{-1}$.
So $P$ cannot be an afterthought — it joins $F_i$ and $T$ as a third unknown profile.

Section 6-5 · summary

Fixed-bed reactor — what to remember

  • Catalyst weight replaces volume: $dF_i/dW=\sum_j\nu_{ij}r'_j$, with $\rho_b=(1-\phi)\rho_c$ and $W=\rho_bA_cz$.
  • Energy, per kilogram: $\sum_j F_jC_{p,j}\,dT/dW=d\dot Q/dW-\sum_i r'_i\Delta H_{ri}$, with $d\dot Q/dW=(Ua/\rho_b)(T_a-T)$ and $a=4/D$.
  • Ergun adds a third ODE that shares unknowns with the others; $\phi$ enters as $(1-\phi)/\phi^3$ and dominates.
  • Hotter feed lights the bed off sooner but expands the gas and costs pressure.
  • Size the bed on the product: here $F_C$ peaks at 0.51 kg and falls after it, while $X_A$ climbs on to 6 kg.

The ceiling

$X$ cannot pass $X_e(T)$, and $X_e$ is thermodynamics, not catalysis.

What dies

The bracket, not $k_1$. A better catalyst arrives at the same ceiling sooner.

Two ways past

Remove a product as it forms, or stage beds with heat exchange between them.

Design guideline

Stop at 90–95 % of the equilibrium conversion

Aim for $X\approx0.90$–$0.95\,X_e$. The rate near equilibrium is the small difference of two large terms, so it dies — and the catalyst needed to buy one more point of conversion runs away.

Move the ceiling instead

Take a product out while the reaction runs and the equilibrium is never reached — reactive distillation, or a membrane reactor.

Or reset the driving force

Cool between adiabatic beds: each bed restarts far from its own ceiling, where the rate is still large.

Where the two curves come from

$X_e(T)$ from equilibrium, $X_{EB}(T)$ from the energy balance

1  $\dfrac{dF_j}{dW}=\nu_j r_1,\qquad F_A=F_{A0}(1-X)$the only equation carrying $W$

2 ÷ 1  $\dfrac{dT}{dX}=\dfrac{-\Delta H_{Rx}F_{A0}}{\sum_j F_jC_{p,j}}$$W$ cancels here, $F_{A0}$ in the next line
$$X_{EB}=X_{in}+\frac{\sum_j\Theta_jC_{p,j}}{-\Delta H_{Rx}}\,(T-T_{in})$$

Section 6-6 · summary

Reversible reactors — what to remember

  • Equilibrium is the rate law at zero net rate: $K_e=k_1/k_2$, so $E_1-E_2=\Delta H_{Rx}$.
  • Exothermic $K_e$ falls with $T$, endothermic rises — one sign decides cooling or reheating.
  • Stop at 90–95 % of $X_e$. Here 0.33 kg reaches 90 %, 0.64 kg reaches 98 %.
  • More catalyst wants a colder feed: optimum $T_F$ 35.7 °C at 0.5 kg, 24.7 °C at 2 kg.
  • Two beds reach 0.907 on 3.41 kg; one bed needs 4.07 kg.

What Problem I asks you to write down

Seven states — five balances, energy and pressure, all over $W$
$$\begin{aligned} \tfrac{dF_{\text{CO}_2}}{dW}&=-r_{s1}-r_{s2} & \tfrac{dF_{\text{H}_2}}{dW}&=-3r_{s1}-r_{s2}\\ \tfrac{dF_{\text{MeOH}}}{dW}&=r_{s1} & \tfrac{dF_{\text{CO}}}{dW}&=r_{s2}\\ \tfrac{dF_{\text{H}_2\text{O}}}{dW}&=r_{s1}+r_{s2} & \tfrac{dP}{dW}&=-\tfrac{fG^2}{\rho\,d_p\,\rho_bA_c} \end{aligned}$$
$$\Big(\textstyle\sum_j F_jC_{p,j}\Big)\frac{dT}{dW}=-r_{s1}\Delta H_{Rx1}-r_{s2}\Delta H_{Rx2}$$
The feed, and the two checks
$$\dot m=GA_c,\quad \bar M=\frac{M_{\text{CO}_2}+5M_{\text{H}_2}}{6},\quad F_{T0}=\frac{\dot m}{\bar M}$$
$$\Phi=R_p\sqrt{\frac{k_s\rho_c}{D_e}},\qquad C_M=\frac{n\,r_{s,\text{obs}}\rho_cR_p}{k_cC_{\text{CO}_2}}$$

Both take $\rho_c$ — each is a balance on one pellet, so both need the rate per pellet volume.

// ks is per kilogram of catalyst; the modulus needs a rate per unit PELLET volume, so it
// is multiplied by the PARTICLE density.
De   = 1.0E-5                                       // [m2/s] effective, in the pores

Phi2 = Rp*(ks2*CatDen/De)^0.5
Eta1 = (3/Phi1^2)*((Phi1/TANH(Phi1)) - 1)
Eta2 = (3/Phi2^2)*((Phi2/TANH(Phi2)) - 1)
CWP  = Eta1*Phi1^2 + Eta2*Phi2^2                    // negligible below 1

Sh = 2 + 0.60*Re^0.5*Sc^(1/3)                       // floor of 2 for a sphere

// ks is per kilogram, so multiply by the PARTICLE density, exactly as Thiele does. Both
// reactions are first order in CO2, so the order n in n*rs*CatDen*Rp/(kc*Cb) is 1.

Propylene oxide, and the two ways to lose it

Problem II · set up first, solve second

Six balances, three criteria, one integral

$$\begin{aligned} \tfrac{dF_P}{dW}&=-r_1'-r_2'-r_3' &\quad \tfrac{dF_{O}}{dW}&=-\tfrac12 r_1'-r_2'-\tfrac92 r_3'\\ \tfrac{dF_{PO}}{dW}&=r_1' &\quad \tfrac{dF_{AC}}{dW}&=r_2'\\ \tfrac{dF_{C}}{dW}&=3r_3' &\quad \tfrac{dF_{W}}{dW}&=r_2'+3r_3' \end{aligned}$$
$$C_j=F_j/v,\qquad Pv=F_TRT,\qquad P_0v_0=F_{T0}RT_0$$

No energy ODE — $T$ is held. The feed is two ratios and one flow, so $v_0$ is a result. Seven states: six flows and the duty.

$$C_{Mears}=\frac{r'}{k_g\,a\,C_O},\qquad a=\frac{6}{d_p\rho_c}$$ $$C_{WP}=\frac{r'\,\rho_c\,(d_p/2)^2}{D_e\,C_O}$$
$$\Delta H_{Rx,i}=\sum_j\nu_{ji}\,\Delta H_{f,j}$$ $$\dot Q=\int_0^{W_{cat}}\sum_i r_i'\,\Delta H_{Rx,i}\;dW$$

Both take $\rho_c$, the particle density — never the bulk density.

Seven algebraic balances, and one line that flips the question

$$F_{j0}-F_j+W_{cat}\sum_i \nu_{ij}\,r_i'=0$$ $$y_j=\frac{F_j}{F_T},\qquad P_j=y_jP_T$$

Well mixed, so every gram sees the outlet gas. No $dW$, no inlet condition, no integration — seven simultaneous equations.

$$x_P=\frac{F_{P0}-F_P}{F_{P0}},\quad Y_{AN}=\frac{F_{AN}}{F_{P0}},\quad S_{AN}=\frac{F_{AN}}{x_PF_{P0}}$$

Key Takeaways

1

Solve the mole and energy balances together — $T$ couples them through $k_j(T)$ and $\Delta H_{Rxj}$.

2

The reactor chooses the mathematics: CSTR algebraic; PFR and bed ODEs in $V$ or $W$; gas beds add Ergun.

3

Plan heat removal before volume — adiabatic exothermic operation invites runaway and multiple steady states.

4

Reversible exothermic? Chase the optimum $T$ — or stage beds with heat exchange.

5

One EQUATRAN skeleton solves all of it: balances, rate laws, then integral … by rkv.
